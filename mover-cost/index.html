    Before you book: check the quote's fine print, check the mover, and see what moving yourself would cost.

    How this works

    By the hour. The total is the rate for the whole crew times the hours billed, plus any travel or truck fee and extras, and never fewer hours than the minimum. If the rate is per mover, it's multiplied by the crew. The hours are our own estimate for your home, the same one our moving day planner uses: about 2.5 minutes of one mover's time for each box and 15 for each big piece, a third more for stairs or an elevator, a fifth more for a long walk, unloading a little faster than loading, movers past four counting half, plus the drive and 30 minutes for walk-throughs. Billed time is rounded up to the quarter hour. The range runs from a fifth faster to a third slower. If the quote names its own hours, you'll see both; you pay for the real time.

    A fixed or binding price. It fixes the cost for the things and services it lists. If you add things, the mover has to write a new estimate before charging more. A mover may charge for writing a binding estimate.

    A non-binding estimate. It's the mover's reasonably accurate guess, and the final bill can be higher. On a move between states, at delivery the mover can't make you pay more than 110 percent of it; anything above that can't be demanded until at least 30 days after delivery. A mover can't charge you for a non-binding estimate.

    For any move between states, the mover must give you a written estimate, say whether it's binding or non-binding, and both of you sign it; you get a dated copy. It's based on a look at your things in person unless you waive that in writing before loading. It can be changed before loading if you both agree, never after.

    What we flag. A quote far below the others (under 60 percent of the next one up), hours on a quote shorter than our low end, and a deposit over a quarter of the likely total. Those lines are our own, not rules. A low price that grows once your things are on the truck is the classic moving scam.

    We don't show any prices of our own: rates vary by city, season and company, and the honest numbers are the ones on your quotes. Local moves within one state follow that state's rules.

    Sources: 49 CFR 375.401, Must I estimate charges? (Cornell Law School LII, law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.401, read November 2026): written estimate before the bill of lading, marked binding or non-binding; a physical survey unless waived in writing before loading; a fee allowed for a binding estimate, none for a non-binding one; reasonably accurate; signed by both, with a dated copy; no changes after loading. The 110 percent and 30-day rules and new estimates for added items: 49 CFR 375.403 to 375.407 and FMCSA, Protect Your Move, as in our answer binding vs non-binding (checked November 2026). Getting several written estimates and the low-price warning: FMCSA, Ready to Move? (as in our answer what a move costs). Hours, the range and the flags are our own estimates.
