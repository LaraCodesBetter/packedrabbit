How this works

The numbers are the government's. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes Regional Price Parities, which compare price levels in each state with the national average of 100. A state at 110 runs 10% above average; a state at 90 runs 10% below.

The math. Your pay, times the new state's number, divided by your current state's number. Rent uses the housing number the same way. Pay is rounded, because this is an estimate, not a promise.

What it leaves out. State and local income taxes aren't counted, so compare take-home pay too. These are statewide averages, and a city can cost well above or below its state. Before you say yes to a job or a lease, look up real rents where you'd live.

Which year. These are 2023 prices, the latest year we could read for all four categories in every state. The bureau's February 2026 release gives 2024 totals, and its highest and lowest states moved by about two points or less (California 110.7, Arkansas 86.9).

Questions people ask

How much do I need to earn to move to another state?

Multiply your pay by the new state's price level, then divide by your current state's price level. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes these price levels as Regional Price Parities. For example, $60,000 in Illinois (98.9) matches about $59,000 in Texas (97.2) and about $68,300 in California (112.6), using 2023 statewide averages and before taxes.

Which states have the highest and lowest cost of living?

In the Bureau of Economic Analysis figures for 2023, California was highest among the states at 12.6% above the U.S. average, followed by New Jersey and Hawaii. The District of Columbia was 10.8% above. Arkansas was lowest at 13.5% below the average, followed by Mississippi and South Dakota.

Does this cost of living comparison include taxes?

No. Regional Price Parities compare the prices of goods, rents, utilities and other services. They don't count state or local income taxes, so compare take-home pay separately before you accept a job or sign a lease.

Is a state average accurate for my city?

It's a starting point. Prices vary inside every state, and rent can differ a lot between a city and the towns around it. Check real rents in the place you're moving to before you decide.
