Moving to Michigan: utilities and your first-month checklist

Which company serves your new address depends on where in Michigan it is. Each link below goes to that company's own page for starting service. Start it two to three weeks ahead, from the day you get the keys.

Water in Michigan

Usually run by the city, town or county. Search the new address's town name with "water service".

Choosing a supplier

Michigan lets some customers choose who supplies their gas. The wires and pipes stay with the utility. Compare on the state's own page before signing anything a door-to-door seller offers: official shopping page.

Help with the bills

If deposits or bills are a stretch, Michigan runs energy assistance: official assistance page. Ask the utility about deposit waivers too; many waive the deposit with a good payment letter from your old company.

Problems with a utility

The Michigan Public Service Commission regulates utilities in Michigan and takes complaints.

Your first month in a new state

  • USPS: change your address: Start mail forwarding up to 30 days early. The real site is usps.com.
  • Your new state's motor vehicle office: Driver's license, registration and title. Most states set a short deadline after you move, so check yours first.
  • Register to vote: Routes you to the official registration for the new state.
  • Medicaid, if you have it: Medicaid doesn't move with you. You apply again in the new state.
  • Health insurance: A permanent move can open a special enrollment period.
  • Social Security: Change your address once here; Medicare gets it from Social Security.
