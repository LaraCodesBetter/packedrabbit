Moving to Connecticut: utilities and your first-month checklist

Which company serves your new address depends on where in Connecticut it is. Each link below goes to that company's own page for starting service. Start it two to three weeks ahead, from the day you get the keys.

Choosing a supplier

Connecticut lets some customers choose who supplies their electricity. The wires and pipes stay with the utility. Compare on the state's own page before signing anything a door-to-door seller offers: official shopping page.

Help with the bills

If deposits or bills are a stretch, Connecticut runs energy assistance: official assistance page. Ask the utility about deposit waivers too; many waive the deposit with a good payment letter from your old company.

Problems with a utility

The Connecticut Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) regulates utilities in Connecticut and takes complaints.

New-resident deadlines in Connecticut

  • Driver's license: within 90 days of moving. Official page
  • Car registration and title: within 90 days of moving. Official page
  • Inspection: Emissions test for vehicles older than four model years. Newer vehicles need a VIN check instead. Official page
  • Connecticut driver license office
  • Register to vote in Connecticut

From the state's own websites, checked October 2026. Deadlines can change; check the official page before you rely on one.

Your first month in a new state

  • USPS: change your address: File up to 90 days before you move. The real site is usps.com.
  • Your new state's motor vehicle office: Driver's license, registration and title. Most states set a short deadline after you move, so check yours first.
  • Register to vote: Routes you to the official registration for the new state.
  • Medicaid, if you have it: Medicaid doesn't move with you. You apply again in the new state.
  • Health insurance: A permanent move can open a special enrollment period.
  • Social Security: Change your address once here; Medicare gets it from Social Security.
