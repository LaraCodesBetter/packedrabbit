Choosing a supplier

New York lets some customers choose who supplies their electricity and gas. The wires and pipes stay with the utility. Compare on the state's own page before signing anything a door-to-door seller offers: official shopping page.

Help with the bills

If deposits or bills are a stretch, New York runs energy assistance: official assistance page. Ask the utility about deposit waivers too; many waive the deposit with a good payment letter from your old company.

Problems with a utility

The New York State Department of Public Service regulates utilities in New York and takes complaints.

New-resident deadlines in New York

  • Driver's license: within 30 days of moving. Official page
  • Car registration and title: within 30 days of moving. Official page
  • Inspection: Not needed before registering. Annual safety inspection, with an emissions check for most vehicles. A valid inspection from your old state counts until it expires or one year after you register, whichever comes first. Official page
  • New York driver license office

Your first month in a new state

  • USPS: change your address: Start mail forwarding up to 30 days early. The real site is usps.com.
  • Your new state's motor vehicle office: Driver's license, registration and title. Most states set a short deadline after you move, so check yours first.
  • Register to vote: Routes you to the official registration for the new state.
  • Medicaid, if you have it: Medicaid doesn't move with you. You apply again in the new state.
  • Health insurance: A permanent move can open a special enrollment period.
  • Social Security: Change your address once here; Medicare gets it from Social Security.
