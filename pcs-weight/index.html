How this works

The allowance. The pounds for each grade are the weight allowance table printed in "It's Your Move," the Defense Transportation Regulation's guide for people moving on PCS orders (July 2023). It's the most your household goods can weigh, across every shipment and any storage, before the extra is charged to you. The Joint Travel Regulations set the rule and your orders and shipping office decide your case: an overseas move to government housing, or a short tour, can come with a lower limit.

Dependents. "With dependents" means at least one family member who can travel at government expense on your orders.

Pro-gear. Professional books, papers and equipment you need for your duties don't count against the allowance when they're declared when you book and weighed: up to 2,000 pounds for you and up to 500 for a spouse. Pack them apart and mark them. Furniture, sports gear, awards and memorabilia, and personal computers aren't pro-gear.

Your estimate. Counting rooms uses the guide's own rough rule of about 1,500 pounds a room, not counting bathrooms. A full garage, shed, attic or storage unit is counted the same, as our own assumption. Big appliances, a piano or a gym's worth of weights add more. If you have a number from a room-by-room count or a past move, type it instead.

If you're over. You pay all the charges for the excess weight. The guide says they can run from several hundred to several thousand dollars, and the bill can come many months after the move. Sell, give away or leave things before the packers come, and ask your shipping office about a reweigh if the weight looks wrong.
