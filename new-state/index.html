    How this works

    Each state sets its own deadlines. Most give new residents a set number of days to get a license and to title and register a car, often 30, 60 or 90. A few set no number and expect it as soon as you become a resident. This page adds the state's number of days to the date you type and shows the date.

    When the clock starts. States count from when you "become a resident" or "establish residency", which usually means moving in, but some count from taking a job there, enrolling children in school, or when the vehicle enters the state. If yours does, it's in the note. Commercial license holders often have less time.

    Before you go. Bring your old license, proof of your new address and your car's title or registration. If the car has a loan, the lender may hold the title; ask your new state how to transfer it. Some states need an inspection first. Book the visit early: appointments can be weeks out.

    Vote at your new address. When you move to a new state, you register to vote there. Each state sets a cutoff before each election.

    These rules change. Every state below links to the official page the rule came from: check it before you rely on a date. Not legal advice.
