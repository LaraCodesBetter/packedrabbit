How this works

The total. Personal property coverage pays to repair or replace your things if they're damaged, destroyed or stolen. So the amount to ask for is what it would cost to replace everything you own. This page adds up what you type and rounds it up to the next $1,000, our own round number.

Replacement cost or actual cash value. Actual cash value is the replacement cost minus depreciation. The Wisconsin insurance commissioner's example: furniture that cost $500 and lasts 20 years, destroyed after 10, pays $250. This page works out the same share for everything at once from the two averages you pick, so it's only a rough guide: an insurer goes item by item. Replacement cost pays the new price, usually once you've bought the item and sent the receipt. It costs more, and pays more. Both pay after the deductible, and a higher deductible usually means a lower premium, so pick one you could pay.

Jewelry, art and collections. Jewelry, cash, guns and stamp and coin collections are usually covered on a limited basis only, and art and antiques should be listed separately. Ask what the policy's limit is, and about a rider or floater for anything above it.

The other parts of a policy. Liability pays if someone is hurt in your home or you damage someone else's property, and many policies pay extra living costs if your home can't be lived in. Ask what each one pays; they aren't part of this total. Most renters policies don't cover floods: see does renters insurance cover floods.

Moving? The landlord's insurance covers the building, not your things. Belongings are covered away from home when the cause is one the policy covers, so ask whether that includes a moving truck and a storage unit, and what the mover's own protection pays: see what your mover pays if something breaks. Have the policy start the day you get the keys.

Keep the list. State insurance departments suggest a list by room with photos or video, receipts, model and serial numbers, with a copy kept online or somewhere safe, and updated once a year. These are estimates, not quotes; prices and policies vary by state and insurer.
