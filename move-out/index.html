How this works

What the list is built from. Tenant guides from legal aid groups say much the same thing: leave the place reasonably clean, with nothing left behind; compare it to how it was when you moved in; take dated photos or video once it's clean; give the landlord your new address in writing; and hand back every key on time. The list puts those steps in order and dates them back from your key day.

Reasonably clean, not spotless. Take all your things, wipe surfaces like counters and the stove, and sweep up. You don't have to scrub top to bottom. Trash or belongings left in or around the place can be charged against the deposit; regular trash put out for pickup is fine.

Normal wear and tear isn't yours to pay for. Faded paint, a few small nail holes and carpet worn where people walk are what ordinary living does. Large holes, burns, stains, broken windows and pet damage are damage. If you caused damage, fixing it yourself before you leave may cost less than the deduction.

Photos, then the walk-through. Clean and empty first so problems can be seen. Photograph every room, inside closets and cabinets and every appliance, before you close the door the last time, and keep them until the deposit is back. If the landlord will walk through with you, point out anything that was there when you moved in. Hand back the keys by the agreed date, with a witness if you can.

The times are our rough estimate for one person cleaning an emptied home: about 10 to 30 minutes a job, longer for the kitchen and with pets. The dates are our suggestions, counted back from your key day. Your lease comes first: some ask for things like professional carpet cleaning or a set notice period. Deposit rules, deadlines and interest vary by state. Not legal advice.
