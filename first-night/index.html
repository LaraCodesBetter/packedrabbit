Your first-night list

Keep an Open First box in the app

In the app, the Open First box gets its own number and photo, so on the first night you type "towels" and know which box. No account, and it works with no signal.

How this works

Rides with you. Anything you'd be stuck without if the truck were a day late: IDs, keys, papers, medicines, glasses, phones and chargers. If you hired a mover, keep the estimate, bill of lading and inventory with you too; you'll need them at delivery. What can't go on the truck at all.

The Open First box. One box, marked on every side, loaded last so it comes off first, or carried in the car. It holds what the first night and morning need before anything else is unpacked.

Amounts. Days to pack for are the nights until your things arrive, plus one. Medicines: at least a week's worth, or the days to pack for plus three. Pet food: two spare days. Baby things: one spare day. Toilet paper: about a roll for every two people a day, never fewer than two. These are our own suggestions, not rules; change them to suit you.

Where it comes from. Ready.gov's emergency kit list covers most of the same ground: water, food, a flashlight, a first aid kit, a wrench or pliers, a manual can opener, a phone with chargers and a backup battery, medicines, glasses, pet food and water, cash and copies of documents, a blanket for each person, a change of clothes and sturdy shoes. It notes that in an emergency it can be hard to refill a prescription or find an open pharmacy. Its rule of one gallon of water a person a day is for an emergency kit; at the new place, the taps will usually do once the water is on.

Flying? Check your airline's rules for liquids, medicines and spare batteries before you pack your carry-on.

Sources: Ready.gov, Build A Kit (Basic Disaster Supplies Kit and Additional Emergency Supplies; read November 2026). FMCSA, Protect Your Move moving checklist (keep the moving papers). Which things go in which bag, and every amount, are our own suggestions. Checked November 2026.
