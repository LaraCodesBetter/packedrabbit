A home inventory, before you need one

After a fire, a flood or a break-in, the insurer asks what you had. A home inventory is the answer you made while you could still see it.

Why it's worth an afternoon

State insurance departments and the National Association of Insurance Commissioners recommend making one before anything happens. A list with photos makes a claim faster and more complete, and it shows whether your policy covers what you own. A 2012 NAIC survey, cited by Minnesota's commerce department, found that more than half of consumers had no list of their possessions.

How to make one, room by room

  1. Walk each room with your phone. A photo of each wall, with closets and drawers open, or a slow video where you say what things are.
  2. Add the details for the things that cost the most: brand, model, serial number, when and where you bought it, and what you paid.
  3. Keep the receipts with it. A photo of each one is enough.
  4. Don't skip the garage, the attic, the basement or a storage unit. Boxes count too.
  5. Keep a copy away from the house: a backup file somewhere else, a fireproof box, or with someone you trust who lives elsewhere.
  6. Update it once a year and whenever you buy something big.

Ask your insurer about the valuable things

Jewelry, art, collections and some electronics may have limits on a standard policy. Ask your agent whether you need extra coverage for them, often called a rider or a floater. Your inventory tells you what to ask about.

Moving is the easiest time to make one

When you pack, you already handle everything you own. A photo of each box before you tape it, with its number and room, is a home inventory you made on the way. It also helps if a mover loses or breaks something: see what a mover pays if something breaks and how to file a claim.

Questions people ask

What is a home inventory?

A record of what you own, room by room, with photos and the details of valuable things, kept so you can show an insurer what you had after a loss.

What should a home inventory include?

Photos or video of every room, closets and drawers open; brand, model, serial number, purchase date and price for valuable items; and photos of receipts. Include the garage, attic, basement and any storage unit.

Where should I keep my home inventory?

Somewhere other than the house as well as on your phone: a backup file stored elsewhere, a fireproof box, or with someone you trust who doesn't live with you.

How often should I update a home inventory?

Once a year, and whenever you buy something valuable.
