What it actually does

Says the box, and it's filed

"A medium Home Depot heavy duty box for the kitchen, fragile, with the stand mixer and the kettle." One sentence. It comes back numbered, sized, weighed and labeled, while both your hands are still on the box.

Measures the doorway for you

Tells you whether the sofa goes through straight, on its side, or not at all — and exactly how many inches short you are before you lift anything.

Works out the truck

Cubic feet from real inside dimensions, with packing slack built in. You measured everything before they quoted, so the quote matches reality.

Answers, in your own words

"Where's the coffee maker?" "What size truck do I need?" "Have I backed up?" It reads your own move and never invents an answer.

Guards the paperwork

The bill of lading, the inventory, the valuation choice, the claims deadline. It won't let you call the move finished while a box is still missing.

Doesn't punish a bad day

Set the day to red and the list shrinks to what genuinely can't wait. Nothing gets marked late for it. Tomorrow picks up where you left off.

On the phone

Your things stay yours

No account needed. Everything lives on your own phone — your medications, your documents, your inventory, your money. We cannot read it because we never receive it.

No analytics, not even anonymous ones. Account numbers are stored as the last four digits only. Share a move with someone and the medical, insurance, financial and document categories never go with it, whatever else you tick.
