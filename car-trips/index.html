    How this works

    Space. A small box is about 1.5 cubic feet, a medium about 3, a large about 4.5, a bag or suitcase about 2.5, and a small piece of furniture (a lamp, a side table, a desk chair) about 6. These are added up and compared with the room your car has for boxes.

    Room in the car. The figure a carmaker lists for cargo space is measured to the roof and into every corner. Square boxes can't use all of that, because of the sloped seatbacks, the wheel wells and the back window, and you need to see out. This counts about 6 of every 10 listed cubic feet as room for boxes, and up to the top of the sides in a pickup bed. The figures for each kind of car are our own rough typical numbers; yours is in your owner's manual. With nobody riding along, the front seat holds about two more medium boxes.

    Weight. Every car and pickup sold in the U.S. has a tire and loading information placard on the edge of the driver's door or its frame, and federal safety standard 110 requires it to state the most that people and cargo together may weigh. Type that number and this takes off about 150 pounds for each person in the car (our own round figure; use the real weight if you know it). Boxes are counted at about 25 pounds for a small or medium, 20 for a large, bag or small piece of furniture. Books and dishes weigh more.

    Driving. Every load but the last is a round trip, because the last one ends at the new home. Each load also gets about 40 minutes to carry out, pack in, and carry in at the other end (our own estimate; stairs and long walks add more). The cost a mile is the IRS's 2026 rate for running costs only, gas and upkeep, unless you choose the full rate or your own.

    Packing the car. Heaviest boxes on the floor, low and forward; light ones on top. Nothing loose on the back shelf or behind anyone's head, and keep the view in your mirrors clear. In a pickup, strap the load down and cover it. Put the box you'll open first in last.
