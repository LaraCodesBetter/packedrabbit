    How this works

    Hazardous materials. FMCSA, the federal agency for interstate movers, lists them as explosives, compressed gases, flammable liquids and solids, oxidizers, poisons, corrosives and radioactive materials. Its examples: nail polish remover, paints, paint thinners, lighter fluid, gasoline, fireworks, oxygen bottles, propane cylinders, automotive repair and maintenance chemicals, and radio-pharmaceuticals.

    • Federal law forbids shipping them in your household goods boxes or luggage without telling your mover. A violation can bring five years in prison and penalties of $250,000 or more (49 U.S.C. 5124).
    • You could also lose your things to fire, explosion or contamination, and packing them without telling the mover can limit what the mover owes you.
    • There's an exception for small amounts, up to 70 ounces total, of medicinal and toilet articles packed in your household goods, and certain smoking materials carried on your person. Ask your mover for details.

    Moving it yourself. Federal hazardous materials rules don't cover people moving their own things for personal use, even in a rented truck, according to PHMSA. That doesn't make it safe, and rental agreements often ban some of these. Read yours.

    Getting rid of it. Most towns run household hazardous waste days or drop-off sites. Search your county's name with "household hazardous waste". Never pour paint or chemicals down a drain or put them in the trash.

    Valuables. If something worth more than $100 a pound, like jewelry, silverware, china or antiques, goes on a mover's truck, list it in writing on the shipping papers, or the mover can limit what it pays for it. See what a mover pays if something breaks.

    Which things should ride with you is our own advice, not a rule. Your mover may refuse more than the federal list, so ask for its list too; it's usually in the paperwork. Not legal advice.
