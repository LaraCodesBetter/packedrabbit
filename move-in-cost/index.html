      How this works

      The total. It adds the rent due at signing (one month, or two if the last month is due too), the deposit, every fee you type, the cost of getting there and first things for the new place, then adds the cushion you choose on top. The starting cushion of 10% is our own suggestion, not a rule.

      The 30% line. HUD considers households that spend more than 30% of income on housing costs to be cost burdened. The Census Bureau, citing HUD, counts rent, mortgage payments and other housing costs, and calls more than 50% severely cost burdened. This page counts rent plus the utilities you pay, against income before taxes. It is a yardstick, not a rule: a landlord may use its own income test, and debts, child care and travel to work matter too.

      How common it is. The Census Bureau reported that nearly half (49.7%) of renter households spent more than 30% of income on housing costs in 2023.

      A first month that starts mid-month. Some leases charge only the days left in the first month, and some ask for the next full month too. Type what your lease says into the rent box, or add the difference under other move-in fees.

      The deposit comes back, later. A security deposit is meant to be returned, less what the law and lease allow, after you move out. Some states limit how large it can be, and each sets a deadline for returning it. See when a deposit is due back in your state.

      Sources: the 30% cost-burden line from HUD Office of Policy Development and Research, Defining Housing Affordability (August 14, 2017); the 50% severe cost-burden line, what counts as housing costs, and the 49.7% share of renter households in 2023 from U.S. Census Bureau, Nearly Half of Renter Households Are Cost-Burdened (September 12, 2024). The starting amounts, counting utilities as housing costs, and the 10% cushion are our own placeholders. Checked November 2026.
