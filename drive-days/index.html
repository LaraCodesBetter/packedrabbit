    How this works

    Days are miles divided by a driving day. Your hours behind the wheel times your average speed is the miles you cover in a day. The last day is whatever is left.

    Stops are added on top, as our own estimates: a 15-minute stop after every 2 hours of driving for fuel and stretching, and a 45-minute meal stop on any day with more than 4 hours of driving. With pets or young children it's a 20-minute stop every 90 minutes. Driving ends by 9 PM, so a late start makes the first day shorter.

    Starting speeds are our estimates of the average while moving, not a speed limit: about 60 mph for a car, 55 for a loaded moving truck and 50 for a truck towing a car. Change it to match your route. Mountains, cities, weather and road work all slow you down, so treat every number here as an estimate and leave room in the plan.

    How many hours is safe? There's no official limit for private drivers. For scale, federal rules let a professional truck driver drive at most 11 hours after 10 hours off, with a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving. They're trained, and you may be in a vehicle you've never driven. Plan well under that, and switch drivers if you can.

    Sleep matters more than miles. CDC says adults need at least 7 hours, so don't start a long driving day after loading the truck late into the night. Pull over and rest, or switch drivers, at the first signs of drowsy driving: yawning or blinking a lot, not remembering the last few miles, missing your exit, drifting out of your lane or hitting a rumble strip.
