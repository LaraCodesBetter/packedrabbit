    How this works

    Call a week ahead, and earlier for internet. Maryland's Public Service Commission says most utilities want at least 72 hours' notice to start, stop or transfer service. Pennsylvania's utility commission tells customers to give at least 7 days' notice before moving to have service stopped. Internet usually needs someone to come out and install it, so this page puts that call three weeks ahead. The 7 and 21 days are our own suggestions, a little early on purpose.

    On the day before, off the day after. This page starts each service at the new place the day before your first night there, and stops it at the old place the day after you leave, so neither home is dark while you move. You pay for both for a few days. If there's a gap between leaving and arriving, nothing needs to be on in between.

    Until you call, it's your bill. Maryland's commission says the utility will keep billing you until you tell it you're leaving or a new customer signs up. Pennsylvania's says you may have to pay for service at the old address even after you move, and suggests keeping proof you gave notice: the date, the time and the name of the person you spoke to.

    The last reading. Pennsylvania's guide says to let the utility reach the meter for a final reading; if it can't, it may bill your last month from an estimate. Writing down and photographing each meter on your last day is our own suggestion, so you can check the final bill.

    Deposits. A utility may ask a new customer for a deposit. In Maryland it's in most cases equal to two months of service, with at least 8 weeks to pay it if it's over $50 and under $150, and 12 weeks over $150; people 60 and older can be exempt with proof of age. In Pennsylvania it can't be more than two months of estimated use, and the utility may check your credit. Maryland returns it with interest after 12 months of on-time bills, or when you close the account, and moving within the same company's area doesn't count as closing it. In Pennsylvania it earns interest and can be held until you've paid on time for 12 months in a row, or 24 months at most. Other states have their own rules; ask.

    What to have ready. Maryland's commission says a utility may ask for your name, phone number, employer, the new address and start date, your old address and move-out date, your date of birth and driver's license number, and an address for the final bill. Pennsylvania's says a utility can ask for a photo ID, and may ask for a Social Security number but can't require one.
