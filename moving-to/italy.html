Moving from the US to Italy: customs, papers and what you can't pack

Bringing your things in duty-free

The official pages we could read are written for Italian citizens returning home: registered abroad (AIRE) for 12 months, goods owned and used personally, imported within 12 months. If you're not an Italian citizen, ask an Italian consulate before you ship — the customs agency's own page wasn't readable when we checked.

The papers customs wants

  • A signed self-declaration (autocertificazione) with the total value in euros
  • A packing list in Italian, with values
  • Passport and codice fiscale (tax code)

Inspection and clearance

Not stated on the pages we could read. Ask your mover and the consulate, in writing.

Don't pack these

  • Meat and dairy from outside the EU are banned. Plants need a phytosanitary certificate.
  • Honey up to 2 kg; fish up to 20 kg
  • Cash of €10,000 or more must be declared.

Bringing a car

For Italian citizens, a car owned 6+ months can come in free. Title and registration must be notarized, apostilled and translated.
