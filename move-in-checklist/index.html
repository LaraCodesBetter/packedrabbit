How this works

    
Why do it at all. When you move out, the landlord can take money from the deposit only for damage beyond normal wear and tear that happened while you lived there. A dated list and photos from the day you moved in show what was already there. Tenant guides from states and legal aid groups all say the same: check every room before you unpack, write down every scratch, stain and thing that doesn't work, however small, and keep a copy.

    
What the list covers. Walls and ceilings, floors, doors and locks, windows, screens and blinds, lights and outlets, the heat and air conditioning, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, every appliance, and every faucet, drain and toilet. Run the water hot and cold, flush, open every door and drawer, and turn on every burner. The rooms and extras you pick add their own rows.

    
Send it in writing, and keep a copy. Give the landlord the report, ask them to sign it if they will, and keep it with your photos until the deposit is back. Email gives you a date stamp. Ask in writing for repairs to anything that doesn't work. Our suggestion is to send it within the first few days; your lease or your state may set its own deadline.

    
Some states have their own rules. In Massachusetts, a landlord who takes a security deposit must give you a statement of condition within 10 days of the tenancy starting or the deposit being paid, and you have 15 days to return it with any damage added. In Washington, a landlord can't collect a deposit unless there's a written checklist of the condition, signed and dated by the landlord and tenant, with a copy to the tenant. California's Department of Real Estate publishes a free inventory checklist for move-in and move-out. Rules in other states differ; check your state's current law. Not legal advice.

Questions people ask
What should I check when I move into an apartment?
Before you unpack, go room by room and check walls and ceilings, floors and carpet, doors and locks, windows, screens and blinds, lights and outlets, every appliance, faucets, drains and the toilet, the heat and air conditioning, and the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Write down every scratch, stain, leak or thing that doesn't work, however small, and take dated photos or a video of every room, including inside the oven, fridge and cabinets.
Should I send my move-in checklist to my landlord?
Yes. Give the landlord a copy in writing, ask them to sign it if they will, and keep a copy with your photos until the deposit is back. Email gives you a date stamp. Ask in writing for repairs to anything that doesn't work. Some states set rules and deadlines: in Massachusetts, a landlord who takes a deposit must give a statement of condition within 10 days, and the tenant has 15 days to return it with any damage added. In Washington, a landlord can't collect a deposit without a written checklist of the condition, signed and dated by both, with a copy to the tenant. Check your state's rules.
Why does a move-in checklist matter for my security deposit?
When you move out, the landlord can only take money from the deposit for damage beyond normal wear and tear that happened while you lived there. A dated move-in list and photos show what was already there, so you aren't charged for it. Use the same list again at move-out.
