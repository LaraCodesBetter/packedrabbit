Check your mover before you pay.

Two steps: the mover's federal record, then ten questions about your estimate and contract. It takes about five minutes and can save thousands.

1. The federal record

Every interstate mover must have a USDOT number. It's usually printed on the estimate, the website and the truck.

Open the official record

Opens the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's own page. We don't see what you look up.

On that page, check three things

  1. USDOT Status says ACTIVE, and "Out of Service Date" says None.
  2. Operating authority includes "Motor Carrier of Household Goods." If it says only "Broker of Household Goods," the company doesn't own trucks. It sells your move to another company, so ask in writing which one.
  3. The name matches your paperwork. A different legal name can be fine (many movers use a trade name), but it should be listed there as the "DBA name."

Then look up the mover's complaint history through the federal Protect Your Move site (choose "Search Movers"), and keep the federal booklet Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move handy. Your mover must give it to you.
