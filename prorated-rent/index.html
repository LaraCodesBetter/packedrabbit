How this works

Prorated rent is rent for part of a month: you pay only for the days you live there. It comes up when a lease starts or ends on a day other than the day rent is due.

Your lease decides the method. There are three common ways to work out a day of rent, and they give slightly different amounts:

  • Days in that month: the month's rent divided by the days in that month (28 to 31). A day costs a little more in February.
  • 365-day year: a year of rent (12 months) divided by 365 days, so every day costs the same all year.
  • 30-day month: the month's rent divided by 30, whatever the month.

Some leases don't prorate at all and ask for a full month, and some ask for the prorated part plus the next full month at signing. Read the rent section of the lease before you pay, and get the amount in writing.

Which days count. Moving in, the move-in day through the last day of that month is counted. Moving out, the first of the month through the last day the place is yours is counted. If your rent is due on a day other than the 1st, ask how the landlord counts the partial period. A move-in on the 1st, or a move-out on the last day of the month, is a full month with nothing to prorate.

The math. Rent times the days counted, divided by the days in the method. Amounts are rounded to the cent and are estimates, not a bill. Rules on rent and fees vary by state and city; check your state's tenant guide at hud.gov/states or your state attorney general. Not legal advice.
