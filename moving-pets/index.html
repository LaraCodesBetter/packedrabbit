    How this works

    Another state has its own rules. The USDA says each state sets its own entry requirements for pets, and they may include a vet's health certificate, up-to-date shots, tests or treatments; some states are much stricter than others. This page can't tell you your state's rules. It tells you when to look them up, on the USDA's state-to-state page, and to ask your vet when the visit should be, because a health certificate is only good for a set time.

    The vet visit. The American Animal Hospital Association suggests a check-up before the move, with any vaccinations that are due, a health certificate if one is needed, and any medicine your pet may need on the way. If your pet gets carsick or anxious in the car, it says to talk to your vet before the move. This page puts that visit three weeks out, with time to go back.

    The carrier, weeks ahead. The same guide says to bring out the carrier gradually, starting weeks before the move, with short car rides to build up to the long one, and to keep pets in a well-ventilated carrier or crate in the car, never loose. This page starts four weeks out.

    Chip and tags before you go. Update the microchip registration with the new address and your contact details, and the ID tags with your current phone number and a backup contact, so whoever finds a pet that slips out can reach you. Tell your vet the new address too. A cell number on the tag works at both homes.

    Moving day. Keep pets away from open doors: a closed room or crate, a friend's place, or boarding for the day. Keep feeding, walks and play on their usual times as much as you can.

    The new place. Start them in one room or area with their familiar bedding and toys, and let them explore the rest only with you there, with doors and windows closed. Block gaps where a pet could get stuck or hurt while you unpack. Some clinginess, hiding or a smaller appetite is common at first; change routines slowly.

    If someone else takes them. A business paid to transport pets is regulated by the USDA. Be careful with anyone who keeps asking for new fees once the pet is on the way; see is it a pet-shipping scam?

    The dates on this page (six weeks for the rules, four for the carrier, three for the vet, two for medicine refills, one for the chip and tags, three days for the bag) are our own suggestions, a little early on purpose.
