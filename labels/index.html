    How this works

    A number for every box, in order. Each room has a short code and its boxes are numbered from 001: KIT-001, KIT-002 and on. University move teams use the same idea: a numbering system set up before the move, numbers in sequence, and every piece labeled. A number is quicker to call out on a busy day than a list of contents, and it's the line you look up later.

    The room it goes to, and how many there are. The room on the label is where the box goes in the new home. Box 3 of 10 means anyone unloading can tell when a room is complete, or that one is still on the truck.

    On the side, not only the top. Once boxes are stacked, the tops disappear. One university relocation guide tells people to put labels on the side and says they must still show when boxes are stacked. Print two a box if you want one on a side and one on an end.

    Write a few words of contents, and keep the list. The last page is a box list with every number and a blank line for what's inside. Fill it in as you seal each box and keep it with you, not in a box. Tick fragile, this side up or heavy on the label where they apply.

    Colors help, words do the work. Each room has its own color band, so a carrier can spot the kitchen boxes from across the room. The room name and number are always printed too, so the labels still work in black and white and for anyone who can't tell colors apart. Room codes, colors and label layouts are our own choices.

    Printing. The labels fit US letter paper. Print on plain paper and tape them on, or on full-sheet sticker paper and cut along the edges. If your printer adds its own margins, choose "Fit to page" or "Scale to fit".
