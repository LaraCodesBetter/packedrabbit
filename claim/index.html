    How this works

    The deadline. For an interstate move, FMCSA says to file a written claim with the mover within 9 months of delivery. We count 9 calendar months from the date you type. Your bill of lading may state the time too, so check it, and don't wait: photos, receipts and memories are easiest to gather in the first weeks.

    Within one state. The federal claim rules on this page are for interstate moves. A move inside one state follows that state's rules and your contract, so read the contract for a claim deadline and ask your state's consumer protection office. Sending the claim early, in writing, is a safe step either way.

    The mover's clock. Once the mover receives your claim, it has 30 days to acknowledge it in writing (49 CFR 370.5) and 120 days to pay, decline or make a firm offer in writing. If it can't, it must tell you in writing why, at the end of every 60 days after that while the claim is open (49 CFR 370.9).

    What to attach. Photos of the damage and of the box it came in, the inventory with your delivery notes, the bill of lading, and receipts, a repair estimate, or a link to the same item for sale. Keep the damaged item and its packing until the mover has had a chance to inspect it.

    Not legal advice. Rules change. Each rule above names the federal regulation it comes from, linked below, so you can check the current text.
