What is storage-in-transit, and what happens if my stuff stays in storage too long?

Storage-in-transit (SIT) is short-term storage the mover arranges in the middle of your move. It is often used when your new home isn't ready yet. The longest it can last is set in the mover's tariff, so ask for that limit and the daily rate in writing before you agree.

When SIT is about to turn into permanent storage, the mover must tell you in writing at least 10 days ahead. The notice must give the date it changes, state that the mover's liability for loss and damage ends then, say you have nine months to file claims, and explain that your goods will then be under the warehouse's rules. If the mover doesn't send this notice, its liability continues until the day after it finally does.

If the mover is ready to deliver before your agreed dates and you can't take the goods yet, the mover may store them near your new home at its own cost. It must tell you the warehouse name and address right away, and it pays for storage, handling and redelivery until final delivery.

Once your goods go into permanent storage, the protection you bought for the move may no longer apply. Ask the warehouse in writing what it covers before that date.

Source: 49 CFR 375.607, 375.609; FMCSA, Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move · Checked October 2026
