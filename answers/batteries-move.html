How do I pack batteries when moving?

Keep batteries in their original packaging if you can. If they're loose, cover the terminals with masking, duct or electrical tape so they can't touch metal or each other. NFPA says this matters most for 9-volt batteries, which shouldn't sit loose in a drawer or box with other batteries.

Tape the bare terminals of lithium batteries too. EPA advises it to cut the risk of sparks.

Check for damage before packing: bulging or swelling, a smell, heat, smoke, leaks or corrosion. Don't pack a damaged battery. Put it in its own sealed clear plastic bag and take it to a battery or household hazardous waste drop-off. Never put it in curbside trash or recycling.

Keep batteries away from heat. Carry spares, chargers and tool batteries with you rather than in a hot truck.

Source: NFPA, 9-Volt Battery Safety; U.S. EPA, Collecting Household Small Format Damaged and Defective Batteries (June 2026) · Checked November 2026
