Do I need new health insurance and a new doctor after moving to another state?

Often, yes. Marketplace plans and Medicaid are tied to your state, and many Medicare Advantage and drug plans are tied to a service area. A permanent move usually gives you a 60-day special enrollment period to pick a new Marketplace plan. To qualify, you generally need to have had coverage for at least one day in the 60 days before the move. In HealthCare.gov states you can't apply until after you move, so plan for the timing.

Coverage usually starts the first day of the next month after you pick a plan. Keep proof of your old address, your new address, and your prior coverage. Some states run their own marketplace with their own rules. Medicaid doesn't move with you, so apply in your new state and close the old case.

Before you move, get copies of your records and a list of your medications and doses. Use your new plan's directory to find a doctor, and call to confirm they are taking new patients. Ask your pharmacy whether it can transfer your prescriptions to a location near your new home.

Source: KFF, How does the permanent move special enrollment work? · Checked October 2026
